# Asking a doctor for feedback

**Why:** feedback from a doctor (a pulmonologist, a cardiologist, or a family doctor) is the most credible next step we can take before patient testing. The team sends this itself. Fill in the brackets, and keep the claims exactly as written: each one is something we have measured.

## Email draft

> **Subject:** Student project on finger clubbing: 15 minutes of feedback?
>
> Dear Dr. [name],
>
> We're [names], students at [school], building a phone app called Nail Vitals for the HOSA Medical Innovation competition. It measures the three published signs of digital clubbing from a side photo of the index finger:
> - the profile (Lovibond) angle;
> - the hyponychial angle;
> - the phalangeal depth ratio.
>
> It flags a result using the cut-offs reviewed by Myers and Farquhar (JAMA 2001), and every result says: "This tool flags a pattern that may be worth discussing with a doctor. It does not diagnose any condition."
>
> So far we have tested it on healthy volunteers and on 3D-printed model fingers with known shapes. Repeat scans of the same finger usually agree within about 3°, so far measured on one person; more people are being added. We have not tested it on patients.
>
> Would you have 15 minutes, in person, by video or by email, to tell us whether the approach makes clinical sense and what would make it useful or trustworthy to you? We aren't asking for any patient information. If you'd be comfortable with us quoting your feedback in our presentation, we'd ask your permission first and quote you exactly.
>
> Thank you,
> [names, school, HOSA chapter, advisor's name and email]

## One-page feedback form (to print or attach)

**Nail Vitals: clinician feedback** (student project, HOSA Medical Innovation)

1. Do you check for clubbing in practice? How, and how often?
2. Is measuring the three published signs from a side photo a sound approach? What worries you about it?
3. Repeat scans agree within about ±3° for the nail angle. Is that precise enough to be useful? What would be?
4. Would a one-page summary that a patient brings to a visit be useful? What should it show?
5. Should a screening tool like this flag a change over time in the same person, as well as a single high value?
6. What would you need to see before trusting it with patients? (For example: a clinic study, agreement with your own measurements, or FDA review.)
7. Anything we should stop claiming, or start measuring?
8. May we quote your answers, with your name and specialty, in our presentation? ☐ Yes ☐ Yes, without my name ☐ No

## Rules
- **No patient data.** Don't send photos of anyone's hands; send screenshots of the app only.
- **Quotes:** use one only with written permission, exactly as given.
- **Advisor:** copy them on the email.
